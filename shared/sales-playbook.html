Sales Playbook

Updated March 19, 2026 — reflects Strategy Document V1 and the “Expertise on Demand” reframe.

1. What We’re Selling

We’re not selling software or knowledge management. We’re selling expertise installation — the creation of human expertise as something AI can work from and cite.1

One sentence: aswritten lets you install expertise from humans you trust into AI and verify where every claim comes from.2

Tagline: Expertise from humans you trust. Cited by AI, aswritten.

Category: Curated expertise installation — the layer above context engineering that determines not just what AI knows, but how it thinks.3

The key distinction: We don’t sell information. We sell perspective — how to interpret information, the processes, the opinions, the judgment calls that determine how facts become decisions.4 That’s what an expert provides that search and RAG cannot.

Provenance (Section 1) — 4/4 claims grounded, 100% coverage. The positioning statement, tagline, category definition, and perspective/information distinction all trace to the March 19 strategy session. These are the foundational anchors — every other section in the playbook descends from these four claims.

2. The Hooks

The hallucination hook (universal opener)

When you ask your people to use AI, how can you tell if it’s hallucinating?

Works for every audience. Names a universal fear in one sentence. The answer — install expertise, verify with citations — differentiates without explaining the architecture.

The expertise distribution hook (for experts and course creators)

You’ve spent years building expertise. How do your clients’ AIs access it? Right now, they can’t. Their AI searches the internet and averages. Your perspective — what makes you valuable — gets stripped away.

This lands with consultants, course creators, authors, practitioners. It names a problem they feel but haven’t articulated: their expertise doesn’t travel through AI.

The delegation hook (for leaders and managers)

When you hand a task to someone on your team, how much time do you spend explaining context? What if their AI already knew how your organization thinks?

This lands when someone manages a team and is tired of re-explaining organizational context every time someone starts a new task.

The knowledge-trapped hook (enterprise closer)

The most valuable knowledge in your organization was never documented. It lives in their heads.5 No retrieval system can access it because it doesn’t exist as an artifact. We extract it.

Use on Call Two or when the prospect is past basic AI trust and into “how do I scale.”

Provenance (Section 2) — 1/4 hooks grounded, 25% coverage. The hooks are rhetorical sales framing — questions designed to open conversations, not assert organizational facts. The knowledge-trapped hook’s key claim (“never documented, lives in their heads”) traces to the Creation Gap principle (Mar 5 2026, validated by Martin Kess and Mike Sackton). The other three hooks are sales language, appropriately uncommitted.

3. The Archetypes — Who Buys and Why

The expert who distributes

Who: Practitioners, consultants, course creators, authors, thought leaders. Pain: “I keep repeating myself. My knowledge doesn’t travel. I can’t be in every room.” Trigger: They see the demo and immediately envision packaging their expertise — Peter Caron: “Can I record my course series, bundle that knowledge, and provide it to students and practitioners?”6 What we sell: Conversion of their existing expertise (book, course, methodology) into a living, installable, citable perspective. Their expertise becomes distributable through AI. Tier path: Team ($400/month) + creation fee + marketplace revenue share.

The leader who delegates

Who: CEOs, team leads, managers, ops leads. Pain: “I need this person to carry out a role that I would otherwise need to explain extensively.” Trigger: A new hire starts, a contractor comes on, a team member shifts domains. Instead of a week of context-setting, install the role’s perspective. What we sell: Organizational roles as persistent, evolving perspectives.7 The accumulated perspective of how roles are performed in this specific organization. Tier path: Team ($400/month) for team use → Organization ($4K/month + $15K setup) for internal catalogue.

The professional who works with AI daily

Who: Therapists, strategists, writers, consultants, designers, lawyers, accountants, developers. Pain: “I spend the first ten minutes of every AI conversation explaining who I am. My AI has no idea how I think.” Trigger: The twentieth time they’ve re-explained their approach to a fresh AI conversation. What we sell: Personal expertise perspective that grows over time. Their AI gets smarter because their perspective is accumulating. Tier path: Free (install marketplace perspectives) → Expert ($81/month) when they want to build their own.

Provenance (Section 3) — 2/3 archetypes grounded with specific evidence. The Expert archetype is anchored by Peter Caron’s real “aha” moment (Mar 19 2026). The Leader archetype’s role-as-perspective concept traces to the synthetic representatives work (PR 91). The Professional archetype’s pain description is consistent with but not directly cited from a specific memory — it’s a synthesis of the Context Fatiguer archetype (both C1 Tinkerer and C2 Consumer variants).

5. The Conversion Service — Setup That Pays

Turning static expertise into the AI-native format IS the service.8 Two models:

Professional perspective creation (we do it)

  • We take their book, course, training materials, or consulting package
  • We run perspective creation, build the perspective, curate the perspective
  • We hand them a living, installable, citable perspective ready for distribution
  • Revenue: Upfront creation fee + ongoing marketplace percentage

Self-service (they do it)

  • The expert creates the perspective themselves using remember/review tools
  • We provide the platform and individuation pipeline
  • Revenue: Marketplace percentage only

Organization setup ($15K)

  • We create the initial roles and packages that populate their internal catalogue
  • Scoped engagement: interview key people, create organizational perspectives, build role perspectives9
  • Revenue: $15K upfront + $4K/month ongoing

This reframes “setup fee” as “expert perspective creation service.” It’s not a cost — it’s the core value proposition. We turn their static expertise into something AI can work from.

Provenance (Section 5) — 2/2 strategic claims grounded. The perspective creation service model and the organization setup fee both trace to the Mar 19-20 sales strategy session. The key reframe — “setup fee” as “expert perspective creation service” — is a settled decision that positions the professional service as value delivery, not cost.

6. Clay’s Sales Path

Step 1: Expert/small business conversion → Team tier

Target: Individuals and small businesses with a single distributable asset — a book, course, consulting methodology, or body of practice.

Pitch: “Your book is a snapshot. We’ll turn it into a living AI perspective.10 Your expertise becomes installable, citable, and generates revenue through the marketplace.”

Close: Team tier ($400/month) + creation fee + marketplace revenue share.

Why this works for Clay: Bounded, clear value, repeatable. Every expert with a book or course is a prospect. The perspective creation is a defined engagement with a tangible deliverable.

Step 2: Organizational catalogue → Organization tier

Target: Team subscribers who want multiple perspectives, or larger organizations that want an internal expertise marketplace.

Upsell trigger: “You’ve published one perspective. Now imagine every role in your organization has one.”

Close: Organization tier ($4K/month + $15K setup) + on-prem add-ons as applicable.

The hooks: Compliance control, data sovereignty, private catalogue, role-based expertise that persists across people.

7. Discovery Call Flow — The Call IS the First Memory

The sales call is not a pitch followed by a demo. The sales call IS the onboarding.11 You interview the prospect, and that conversation becomes their first perspective.

Before the call (5 min)

  • Send the intro deck (experts/SMB) or one-sheet (enterprise warm intro)
  • Prep: what’s their existing expertise? What asset could become a perspective?

Opening (2 min)

  • Use the appropriate hook for the archetype
  • Expert: expertise distribution hook
  • Leader: delegation hook
  • Professional: hallucination hook
  • Don’t wait for a long answer. Transition: “Let me show you what it looks like.”

Read-path demo (10 min) — three beats

  1. Show installed expertise in action. Use an existing perspective. The AI reasons from the expert’s perspective, not from generic training data.
  2. Show the citations. Every claim traces to a specific person, decision, and context. “Here’s how you know it’s not hallucinating.”
  3. Show the gaps. Run introspect. The system knows what it doesn’t know. “These are the boundaries of what’s installed. That’s where your expertise goes.”

The pivot (5 min)

  • Expert archetype: “What I just showed you is someone else’s expertise installed. Want to see what yours would look like?”
  • Leader archetype: “Imagine your team’s AI knowing this about how your organization works.”
  • Professional archetype: “What if your AI remembered this for every future conversation?”
  • Interview them. This is the first perspective creation.

Close (5 min)

  • “What you just told me — that’s your first perspective content. If we save it, your AI starts knowing this tomorrow.”
  • Expert: “I can convert your [book/course/methodology] into a full perspective. You’d have a living, installable version of your expertise.”
  • Leader: “Let’s get your team on a trial. I’ll set up the first role perspective.”
  • Professional: “The free tier lets you install existing perspectives right now. When you’re ready to build your own, it’s $81/month.”

After the call

  • Save the memory immediately (with permission)
  • Send the pre-populated perspective within 24 hours — do NOT wait
  • Follow up when perspective creation completes: “Your expertise is live. Try asking your AI about [topic from the call].”
  • Peter Caron lesson: the follow-up repo was never sent and activation stalled. Do not repeat this.

Provenance (Section 7) — call flow grounded in Sales-as-Delivery. The “call IS the onboarding” principle traces to the Mar 17 pricing session. The specific call structure (before/opening/demo/pivot/close/after) is new operational guidance from this playbook — it operationalizes the settled principle but the specific timing and scripting is uncommitted. The Peter Caron follow-up lesson is a documented failure (task-165 created to fix it).

8. Objection Handling

“How is this different from a Custom GPT / Claude Project?”

Custom GPTs upload files. That’s locally curated RAG — it makes information accessible, but it doesn’t install perspective.12 There are no opinions, no interpretive frameworks, no “this is how I’d approach your situation.” The files are data, not perspective. And you can’t tell which file influenced which answer.

“How is this different from just doing a web search?”

Search is your AI trying to decide what’s important and finding it for you. This is a human with a lifetime of expertise curating an entire perspective — and giving that to your AI at the point of need in a way it can work from and cite. Search gives you information. This gives you perspective.

“Why not just use RAG?”

RAG retrieves document chunks. aswritten installs perspective — how to interpret those facts, when conventional wisdom is wrong, what the expert would actually think about your situation. RAG gives your AI more context. aswritten gives your AI a way of thinking.

“We already have Notion/Confluence.”

Those are for humans to read. aswritten is for AI to reason from. Different audiences, different structure. Your wiki onboards humans. aswritten onboards every AI tool across your organization simultaneously with structured, citable knowledge.

“What about data security?”

The perspective creator owns their source material at rest in their own Git repo. Consumers receive a published perspective — not the underlying documents.13 You can’t reconstruct the original material from the published output. On-prem available on the Organization tier.

“We’re not ready / too early for this.”

If your team is using AI daily, you’re ready. But you don’t need to build anything first — install a free perspective from the marketplace and see the difference immediately. Build your own when you’re ready.

“$400/month is expensive.”

Mike Sackton at Escher Group: “$400/month isn’t real money.”14 His Copilot overage budget goes up $500/month at a time. Compare to: senior engineer time answering the same questions ($200/hr × 10hrs/week = $8K/month). Or compare to: what you’d pay a consultant to do what the perspective does permanently.

“Why would I pay for a perspective when I can just ask AI?”

You can ask AI and get an answer from its training data — the undifferentiated average. Or you can ask AI backed by an expert’s curated perspective and get an answer that cites their actual reasoning. One is a guess. The other is a consultation.

Provenance (Section 8) — 3/8 objection responses grounded, 38% coverage. The Custom GPT distinction (locally curated RAG, not perspective) traces to Scarlet’s March 19 correction. The data security response (creator owns, consumer gets published artifact) traces to the moat discussion. The pricing objection cites Mike Sackton directly. The remaining five responses are operational sales language — they apply grounded principles (perspective vs. information, the creation gap) but the specific phrasing is new to this playbook.
