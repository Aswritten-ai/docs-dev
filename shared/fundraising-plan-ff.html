aswritten — Gift Round Brief

Date: March 2026

What I’m asking for

$5,000 per month through the end of the summer — $25,000 total.1 This ends when I get into an incubator, close a fundraising round, or reach enough revenue to sustain myself.2 Whichever comes first.

This is not an investment in the business — that comes later, through the incubator or a formal fundraising round.3 This is support for me personally to be able to focus full-time on getting the business to its next milestone.4

The problem

Everyone is using AI now. But AI has a fundamental trust problem that isn’t going away.5

When you ask AI to help with something — write a memo, research a topic, advise on a decision — it produces confident, fluent text. But you have no idea where that answer came from. It could be drawing on solid information or it could be making things up. There’s no way to tell. The answer has no sources, no citations, no provenance.6

This matters more than people realize. Inside organizations, the people who’ve been there for years can steer AI effectively because they already know when it’s wrong.7 Their AI is supercharged by their experience. But junior people, new hires, and anyone without deep context get generic output and can’t tell the difference.8 AI was supposed to level the playing field — instead, it’s widening the gap between people who have expertise and people who don’t.9

Outside organizations, the same problem is emerging for experts. Consultants, practitioners, educators, authors — people who’ve spent careers building deep expertise — are watching that expertise get stripped away.10 When someone asks AI about their field, the AI doesn’t draw on the expert’s decades of knowledge.11 It searches the internet and averages. The expert’s nuanced perspective becomes noise in a training dataset.

The question every expert now faces: how do the people who trust my judgment access it through the AI they’re already using?12 Right now, the answer is: they can’t.

What aswritten does

aswritten solves this. It lets people take human expertise — their own, or the expertise of someone they trust — and install it into AI so that the AI actually thinks the way that expert thinks.13 And when the AI gives an answer, it shows exactly where that answer came from, so you can verify it.14

The key difference is between information and perspective.15 Every existing AI tool — search, document uploads, chatbot memory — gives AI more information.16 But information isn’t what makes an expert valuable. What makes an expert valuable is how they interpret information: the judgment calls, the processes, the opinions earned through practice, the sense for when the conventional wisdom is wrong.17 That’s perspective. aswritten installs perspective, not just information.18

Think of it like this: instead of asking AI a question and getting a generic internet answer, you can ask it a question and get an answer informed by a specific expert’s thinking — and the AI tells you “this came from Dr. So-and-so’s clinical methodology” or “this came from your organization’s decision in January.”

Who it’s for

Three kinds of people need this:

Experts who want their knowledge to scale. Practitioners, consultants, course creators, authors — anyone whose expertise is their value and who can’t be in every room. Peter Caron, a practitioner I’ve been working with, immediately asked: “Can I record my course series, bundle that knowledge, and provide it to students and prescribing practitioners?”19 The answer is yes — and unlike a book or a course, the AI version stays alive and evolves with his thinking.20

Leaders who need their team to work with context they don’t have time to explain. CEOs, managers, team leads — anyone who delegates tasks that require deep context. Instead of spending 30 minutes explaining how the organization thinks about something, they install the organization’s perspective and the team member’s AI already knows.21 New hires don’t start from zero.22

Professionals who use AI every day and are frustrated by how generic it is.23 Therapists, strategists, writers, consultants, lawyers — anyone whose domain expertise is their competitive advantage but whose AI doesn’t reflect it. They spend the first ten minutes of every AI conversation re-explaining who they are and what they’re working on.

The business model

The product has four tiers:

  • Free: Try it. Install expertise from the marketplace. See the value with zero investment.
  • Expert ($81/month): Build your own expertise. Your AI gets smarter over time.
  • Team ($400/month): Distribute your expertise to others through the marketplace. Collaborate with a team.
  • Organization ($4,000/month): Your own private marketplace of organizational expertise.24

We also offer a perspective creation service — we take someone’s existing book, course, or consulting methodology and turn it into the AI-native format.25 This is a professional service with its own fee, and it’s how we bring experts onto the platform.

The product works

The product is built. It works. I’ve been using it every day to build the company itself. The strategy document I wrote this week — a 15,000-word comprehensive business strategy — was written in a single day using the product, and 80% of its claims trace back to documented decisions made over the past 14 months. The product is its own proof of concept.

Books were static knowledge distribution. Search was algorithmic knowledge discovery. aswritten is something new: curated expertise installation — human perspective, installed at the moment of need, cited and verifiable. I believe this is the future of how trusted information works in the age of AI.

Where we are now

The product works. It’s been in beta with a small group of people and the technology is solid. People who see it get excited — they immediately understand the value.

We’re transitioning from beta to a public launch on April 1. After that date, anyone can sign up. There will be a free tier (try it and see the value) and paid tiers for people who want to build their own expertise or distribute it to others.

People are interested but haven’t paid yet. This is the honest reality. We’ve had strong conversations with several potential customers:

The product resonates broadly — the question isn’t whether people see the value, it’s converting that interest into paying users, which is what the April launch is designed to do.

The gap between interest and payment is that people understand the value but see it as something they’ll “set up when they have time.” The April launch changes this — instead of asking people to build their knowledge base from scratch, we’re shipping pre-built expertise packages that work immediately. You sign up, install one, and your AI is immediately better at a specific thing. That’s the hook.

The Y Combinator opportunity

I applied to Y Combinator in February. They’re the most prestigious startup incubator in the world — companies like Airbnb, Stripe, and Dropbox came through YC. They responded that my application was in the top 10% and I should reapply.

That’s a strong signal. What I read between the lines: they love the idea, they think it’s the right thing to build, and they want to see a little more traction before they commit.

The next application round opens in early May, with responses in mid to late June. YC invests approximately $400,000 immediately on acceptance. That would be the ideal amount to hire my first team member and scale the business through the end of the year.

The plan

Now through April 1: Finish preparing the product for public launch. This means updating the website, creating demo materials, finalizing the starter packages that new users will get for free, and making sure someone can sign up and use the product without any technical setup.

April: Public launch. Marketing push. The goal: get a handful of paying customers. Specifically: 1-2 people paying $81/month for the Expert plan, and ideally one team or expert paying $400/month. Even a small amount of paying traction changes the story from “people are interested” to “people are paying.”

Early May: Submit the YC reapplication, plus applications to other startup incubators that accept rolling applications. The traction from April — paying customers, published expertise packages, demo materials — is the evidence they’re looking for.

Mid to late June: YC responds. Other incubators respond. If accepted, the next phase is funded immediately.

If incubators don’t land (July-August): I pursue a formal investment round — a small raise from angels and advisors using a standard startup investment instrument called a SAFE. The paying customers and traction from April-June make that conversation much easier than it would be today.

Worst case: I take a side contract to cover costs while continuing to build. I’ve done this before and it works — it just slows things down.

What the gift enables

$5,000/month covers my living costs and business expenses (hosting, AI tools, software subscriptions). The ask is through the end of August — five months — and it stops early if any of these happen:

  • I get accepted to an incubator (YC invests ~$400K immediately)
  • I close a formal investment round
  • The business reaches enough revenue to cover my costs

Without this, I’m splitting my focus between the business and finding contract work to cover basics. With it, I can go all-in on the launch, the traction push, and the incubator applications during the window when they matter most.

What success looks like

By end of April:

  • The product is publicly available and people are using it
  • At least a few people are paying for it
  • I have demo materials and case studies showing real value

By end of June:

  • YC or another incubator has accepted us (best case)
  • Or: I have enough traction and materials to raise a small investment round (good case)
  • Or: I have a clear path to contract work that supports me while I continue building (baseline case)

In all three scenarios, the business is further along than it is today, with a live product, real users, and a track record. The gift gets me through the window where all of this is possible.

The story

I’ve been building toward this for 15 years. My entire career has been about how organizations communicate their expertise — from my first startup through consulting roles to now. AI changed the game because it’s the first time a technology could actually absorb and apply human perspective, not just store it. I’ve spent the last year and a half building the infrastructure to make that work, and it does work. The product is real, it’s live, and the people who’ve seen it get it.

What I need now isn’t money to build the product — the product is built. I need runway to launch it, prove that people will pay for it, and get through the incubator application window. That’s five months at $5,000/month. After that, the business is either funded or I find another way.

Questions you might have

Why aren’t people paying yet? Because we’ve been in closed beta — only a handful of people have had access. The public launch on April 1 is when we open the door. The people who’ve seen it are excited; they just haven’t had the chance to sign up and pay yet.

What if YC says no again? Then I pursue a small investment round directly, using the traction from April-June. Or I take a side contract and keep building at a slower pace. The business doesn’t stop — it just moves at a different speed.

What’s the risk? The risk is that people don’t convert from “interested” to “paying.” That’s the risk of any new product. But the signal so far is strong, the product works, and the market is real — everyone using AI has this problem. The gift buys me the time to test whether my solution is the one they’ll pay for.

Is this a one-time ask? Yes. $5,000/month through the summer, stopping when I get funded or self-sufficient. After that, the business is either funded or I find another way.

What about the formal investment opportunity? That’s separate and comes later. If the business gets traction and I raise a formal round (either through YC or directly), there will be an opportunity to invest through a standard startup instrument. I’ll share those details when the time comes. This gift is personal support, not a business investment.
